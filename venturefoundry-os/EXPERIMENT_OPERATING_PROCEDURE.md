# VentureFoundry OS™ — Experiment Operating Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure ensures that venture experiments reduce decision uncertainty rather than merely generate activity. It applies to customer discovery, product, pricing, channels, operations, technology, finance, partnerships and impact assumptions.

## 2. Principle

No experiment begins without a decision it is intended to inform.

Every experiment must state:

- the decision owner;
- the assumption under test;
- the expected evidence;
- the threshold for pass, revise or stop;
- the maximum time and cost;
- the material risks and controls;
- the decision that follows each possible result.

## 4. Experiment selection

Prioritize assumptions using:

```text
Assumption Priority = Impact if wrong × Uncertainty × Immediacy
```

Rate each factor from 1 to 5. Test the highest score first unless safety, legal or technical sequencing requires otherwise.

## 5. Approved experiment types

### Discovery experiments

- structured problem interviews;
- observation or contextual inquiry;
- process shadowing;
- existing-data review;
- stakeholder mapping.

### Demand experiments

- landing page with measurable call to action;
- waitlist with qualification;
- pre-order or deposit;
- letter of intent;
- pilot commitment;
- concierge or manual service.

### Product experiments

- clickable prototype;
- wizard-of-oz service;
- feature flag or limited release;
- usability test;
- reliability, performance or security test.

### Commercial experiments

- pricing interview followed by real offer;
- channel test;
- outbound sequence;
- proposal conversion test;
- partner-led sale;
- contract or procurement test.

### Operating experiments

- service simulation;
- capacity stress test;
- supplier trial;
- support workflow test;
- incident tabletop exercise;
- unit-cost measurement.

## 6. Evidence quality controls

- Separate stated preference from observed behaviour.
- Do not count friends, employees or sponsors as representative customers unless they belong to the target segment.
- Record negative evidence and non-response.
- Avoid changing multiple causal variables when a focused test is possible.
- Predefine thresholds before results are known.
- Preserve source records and data lineage.
- Mark estimates, assumptions and verified facts distinctly.
- Obtain consent and protect personal or confidential information.

## 7. Decision thresholds

An experiment is:

- **passed** when the predefined threshold is met with adequate evidence quality;
- **inconclusive** when evidence volume, quality or design is insufficient;
- **failed** when the stop threshold is reached;
- **invalid** when execution deviates materially from the approved method.

A failed experiment is a useful result when it prevents larger waste.

## 8. Timeboxing

Default limits unless approved otherwise:

| Venture stage | Maximum experiment cycle |
|---|---:|
| Opportunity discovery | 5 working days |
| Value proposition | 10 working days |
| Demand and pricing | 15 working days |
| MVP feature or workflow | 10 working days |
| Channel or sales test | 20 working days |
| Scale or operational test | 30 calendar days |

Longer tests must include intermediate evidence checkpoints.

## 9. Weekly review

For each open experiment, review:

- status against due date and budget;
- evidence collected;
- execution validity;
- emerging risk;
- whether the assumption remains decision-critical;
- whether to continue, redesign or stop immediately.

## 10. Learning register

The learning register must contain:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Learning ID | unique and versioned |
| Related assumptions | linked IDs |
| Evidence | direct source or controlled summary |
| Applicability | venture, segment, product or portfolio |
| Confidence | low, medium, high or verified |
| Decision impact | what changed because of the learning |
| Reuse condition | where the learning may or may not be generalized |
| Owner | accountable maintainer |

## 11. Prohibited practices

- retrofitting success thresholds after seeing results;
- presenting vanity metrics as customer value;
- hiding negative evidence;
- running experiments without a decision owner;
- collecting personal or confidential data without a legitimate purpose and control;
- scaling a test before the critical assumption is validated;
- using AI-generated synthetic findings as if they were customer evidence.

## 12. Closure

An experiment closes only when the result, evidence, learning and resulting decision are recorded. Unclosed experiments are operational debt and must be reviewed before new work is authorized.
