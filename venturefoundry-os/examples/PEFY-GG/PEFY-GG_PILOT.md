# PEFY-GG VentureFoundry OS™ Pilot Blueprint

## 1. Pilot objective

Apply VentureFoundry OS to PEFY-GG as the first controlled reference implementation, proving that the group can select, validate, build, govern and scale ventures through one evidence-based portfolio system.

This public-safe blueprint does not contain client-confidential data, personal information, security details or unpublished financial commitments.

## 2. Strategic diagnosis

PEFY-GG has substantial multidisciplinary capability and a broad portfolio of business units, products, platforms, programmes and social-impact initiatives. Its principal opportunity is not generating more ideas. It is creating a disciplined mechanism that:

- distinguishes operating businesses from experiments and strategic options;
- concentrates scarce capital and leadership attention;
- converts expertise into repeatable products and intellectual property;
- establishes one source of truth for evidence, decisions, risks and performance;
- uses AI and automation without losing human accountability;
- builds Africa-first solutions capable of international deployment.

### Primary risk

The portfolio can become overextended when too many initiatives remain simultaneously labelled as priorities. This creates fragmented delivery, weak evidence, unclear ownership, duplicated platforms, delayed monetization and hidden operational debt.

### Pilot response

Use a four-horizon portfolio and authorize only one flagship growth venture for intensive validation during the first 90-day cycle.

## 3. PEFY-GG portfolio architecture

**Decision:** standardize offers, price architecture, evidence packs, sales pipeline and delivery quality. Manage for margin, cash collection, references and cross-selling.

- potential expansion from telecom to construction, energy, logistics, industry and public institutions;
- direct synergy with PEFY-GG consulting, training and audit capabilities.

**90-day decision:** validate a narrow, paid or formally committed pilot before expanding the platform scope.

## 5. Flagship venture thesis: EL-VECTOR

### Mission

Enable organizations to operate OHSE risk, process, audit and compliance management through one evidence-centered, field-ready and management-visible system.

### Priority customer segment

Start with one narrow segment where PEFY-GG has access and expertise, such as multi-site telecom or infrastructure operations requiring contractor control, field risk assessment, evidence and corrective-action tracking.

### Priority problem

OHSE information is often fragmented across spreadsheets, email, paper forms and disconnected documents. Leadership cannot reliably see current risk, overdue actions, evidence completeness or process performance across sites and contractors.

### Initial value proposition

EL-VECTOR provides a controlled operational loop:

```text
Assess risk → assign control/action → capture evidence → verify closure
→ monitor compliance/process performance → escalate and report
```

### MVP boundary

Include only:

- tenant and role access;
- site/project register;
- risk assessment;
- process/control checklist;
- audit or inspection;
- findings and corrective actions;
- evidence upload and traceability;
- basic dashboard and export;
- offline-tolerant field capture where pilot conditions require it.

Exclude from the first pilot unless mandatory:

- broad ERP functionality;
- advanced predictive AI;
- marketplace features;
- complex billing automation;
- every industry template;
- unrestricted customization;
- large-scale integration catalogue.

## 6. Critical assumptions to test

| ID | Assumption | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | OHSE leaders experience material pain from fragmented tools | verified interviews and workflow observation |
| A2 | A narrow end-to-end action/evidence loop is more valuable than a broad document library | prototype task completion and preference |
| A3 | Organizations will commit to a controlled pilot | signed pilot, access, named users and sponsor |
| A4 | Users can complete field tasks with acceptable time and training | observed usability and task-success data |
| A5 | Management dashboards influence escalation and closure | before/after overdue-action and evidence metrics |
| A6 | A viable price combines platform subscription and implementation/support | real proposal, negotiation and commercial commitment |
| A7 | Required privacy, security and tenant isolation can be achieved within pilot cost | architecture and control validation |

## 7. Pilot success metrics

### Evidence and adoption

- at least 15 relevant user/stakeholder interviews;
- one formally authorized pilot organization or business environment;
- 10 or more active pilot users across at least three roles;
- 80% task-completion rate for the five critical workflows;
- median onboarding time below 60 minutes;
- at least 60% weekly active use among assigned pilot users.

### Operational value

- 30% reduction in overdue corrective actions in the pilot scope;
- 90% of closed actions linked to acceptable evidence;
- management reporting preparation time reduced by at least 50%;
- material incidents, risks and escalations visible within agreed reporting time.

### Commercial evidence

- validated buyer, budget owner and procurement path;
- one priced proposal using a repeatable package;
- paid pilot, deposit, purchase order or equivalent formal commercial commitment preferred;
- documented unit-cost and support-effort baseline.

### Quality and trust

- no unresolved critical security, privacy, legal or safety issue;
- complete decision and evidence traceability;
- less than 5% critical workflow failure rate;
- user feedback classified and actioned within one review cycle.

## 9. Decision rules after the pilot

### Scale EL-VECTOR when

- the pilot demonstrates measurable operational value;
- users repeatedly complete the critical workflows;
- a buyer accepts a credible commercial model;
- data, security, support and delivery risks are manageable;
- the next segment can be reached without uncontrolled customization.

### Continue validation when

- user value is present but pricing, retention, workflow or technical evidence remains incomplete;
- one focused cycle can resolve the uncertainty within an approved budget.

### Pivot when

- the problem is real but the primary user, buyer, value loop or delivery model is wrong;
- evidence supports a materially better adjacent thesis.

### Stop or archive when

- no customer commitment is obtained;
- users do not adopt the critical workflow;
- economics depend on excessive bespoke delivery;
- critical risk cannot be controlled;
- continued work is justified mainly by prior effort.
