# PEFY-GG — 90-Day VentureFoundry OS™ Activation Plan

## 1. Outcome

Within 90 days, PEFY-GG will establish a controlled venture portfolio and complete one evidence-based EL-VECTOR pilot cycle capable of supporting a scale, continue, pivot or stop decision.

## 2. Operating constraints

- Maximum three protected group priorities during the activation period.
- EL-VECTOR is the only venture authorized for intensive product validation in this cycle.
- Other initiatives remain cash engines, operating-backbone work or time-boxed strategic options.
- No large build begins without a passed gate and approved budget.
- Client, personal, financial and security-sensitive information remains in controlled repositories, not in this public example.

## 3. Workstreams

1. Portfolio governance and operating backbone.
2. EL-VECTOR discovery and commercial validation.
3. MVP product and technical readiness.
4. Pilot operations and customer success.
5. Finance, risk, compliance and evidence assurance.
6. Learning capitalization and scale decision.

## 4. Phase plan

### Days 1–15 — Mobilize and classify

**Objectives**

- establish authority, scope, roles and records;
- classify all known PEFY-GG initiatives;
- confirm EL-VECTOR's target segment and pilot boundary.

**Actions**

- appoint Executive Sponsor, Portfolio Lead and EL-VECTOR Venture Owner;
- approve the VentureFoundry charter and gate authority;
- create the portfolio register;
- classify each initiative as operating backbone, cash engine, flagship venture or strategic option;
- assign current gate, owner, next evidence, budget status and expiry date;
- identify duplicated platforms, components and data models;
- approve the EL-VECTOR assumption register and discovery plan;
- establish evidence repository, naming, access and version rules;
- set weekly operating review and monthly steering review.

**Deliverables**

- approved portfolio governance note;
- complete portfolio register;
- role/delegation matrix;
- EL-VECTOR charter;
- assumption and risk registers;
- baseline budget and capacity plan;
- evidence repository live.

**Gate**

G0 Mandate passed for EL-VECTOR.

### Days 16–30 — Validate problem, user and buyer

**Objectives**

- verify priority pain and current workarounds;
- distinguish user, buyer, sponsor and procurement actors;
- identify the smallest valuable operational loop.

**Actions**

- conduct at least 15 interviews across OHSE leadership, field users, auditors/inspectors, contractors, IT/security and budget/procurement roles;
- observe or reconstruct at least three current workflows;
- quantify delays, rework, evidence gaps, reporting effort and overdue actions;
- test three value-proposition variants;
- map alternatives, switching costs and adoption barriers;
- identify one pilot sponsor and operating environment;
- record disconfirming evidence and revise assumptions.

**Deliverables**

- evidence-backed problem statement;
- ideal customer profile and role map;
- current-state journey;
- quantified baseline;
- value-proposition decision;
- pilot candidate and decision path.

**Gate**

G1 Opportunity passed only when the problem, target segment, why-now and market-access route are supported by evidence.

### Days 31–45 — Prototype and secure commitment

**Objectives**

- demonstrate the critical workflow before broad development;
- obtain behavioural and commercial commitment.

**Actions**

- design a clickable or low-code prototype for the end-to-end loop;
- run task-based usability tests with representative users;
- measure task success, time, errors and support required;
- test implementation package and pricing architecture;
- present a defined pilot proposal with responsibilities, scope, duration and success metrics;
- complete preliminary security, privacy, legal, IP and architecture reviews;
- negotiate access, named users, sponsor and evidence rights.

**Deliverables**

- validated workflow prototype;
- usability findings and prioritized backlog;
- pilot proposal and commercial package;
- pilot terms or equivalent formal commitment;
- solution feasibility and risk assessment;
- MVP scope and exclusions.

**Gate**

G2 Solution and G3 Validation conditional pass require a formal pilot commitment and no unresolved critical risk.

### Days 46–65 — Build the minimum reliable value loop

**Objectives**

- deliver only the functions required to prove operational value;
- instrument the system for evidence.

**Actions**

- implement tenant and role access;
- implement site/project, risk, checklist/audit, finding/action and evidence functions;
- provide dashboard and controlled export;
- implement minimum offline-tolerant capture if required by field conditions;
- configure audit logging, backups, access review and incident path;
- create pilot templates and controlled master data;
- execute functional, usability, security and recovery checks;
- train pilot administrators and support owners;
- freeze non-pilot features.

**Deliverables**

- pilot-ready MVP;
- architecture and data-flow record;
- acceptance test evidence;
- release/rollback checklist;
- user guidance and support process;
- measurement dashboard;
- approved pilot release.

**Gate**

G4 MVP passed when the complete value loop works, critical tests pass, ownership is assigned and evidence collection is active.

### Days 66–80 — Operate the pilot

**Objectives**

- prove adoption, operational value, reliability and supportability;
- capture real commercial and delivery cost data.

**Actions**

- onboard users by role;
- monitor activation, weekly use, workflow completion, errors and support demand;
- compare overdue actions, evidence quality and reporting time against baseline;
- conduct weekly customer-success review;
- resolve critical defects under controlled change;
- track delivery effort, hosting, support and implementation costs;
- test the buyer's willingness to continue under a priced model;
- capture customer reference subject to approval.

**Deliverables**

- adoption and cohort dashboard;
- operational-value report;
- issue, incident and corrective-action log;
- support and unit-cost baseline;
- commercial continuation evidence;
- prioritized post-pilot backlog.

**Gate**

G5 Traction assessment completed. A score alone cannot pass the gate without user adoption, value and commercial evidence.

### Days 81–90 — Decide and institutionalize learning

**Objectives**

- make an explicit evidence-based decision;
- prepare the next controlled cycle.

**Actions**

- independently review the evidence pack;
- calculate evidence-adjusted venture score and residual risk;
- reconcile budget, commitments, collections and forecast;
- determine scale, continue validation, pivot, hold or stop;
- update portfolio priorities and resource allocation;
- capture reusable product, domain, sales, delivery and governance learning;
- approve V1.1 improvements to VentureFoundry OS;
- publish a public-safe case summary and retain confidential annexes internally.

**Deliverables**

- final pilot report;
- gate decision and conditions;
- 12-month commercial and product roadmap if continued;
- funding and capacity request;
- updated risk and compliance plan;
- portfolio decision register;
- VentureFoundry OS V1.1 change log.

## 5. Weekly operating review

### Required inputs

- prior commitments and completion status;
- experiment results and evidence links;
- product/release status;
- user and commercial metrics;
- spend, commitments and runway;
- top risks, incidents and blockers;
- decisions required.

### Required outputs

- maximum three priorities for the next week;
- owner and due date for every action;
- stop/defer decisions;
- escalations within 24 hours;
- updated decision and learning logs.

## 6. Core dashboard

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| Portfolio | ventures by horizon/gate, owner completeness, expired options, resource concentration |
| Evidence | assumptions tested, pass/fail/inconclusive, confidence, evidence ageing |
| Product | critical workflow completion, defects, release readiness, technical debt |
| Customer | activation, weekly use, retention, task success, satisfaction, support demand |
| Commercial | qualified pipeline, pilot commitment, price acceptance, conversion, collections |
| Finance | budget, actual, committed, forecast, runway, unit cost and margin |
| Risk | top residual risks, overdue treatments, incidents, compliance status |
| Delivery | milestone performance, blockers, capacity and dependency status |

## 7. 90-day acceptance criteria

The activation succeeds when:

- all active initiatives are classified with owner, gate, next evidence and expiry/decision date;
- no more than three priorities consume protected leadership capacity;
- EL-VECTOR completes at least G4 and enters a controlled pilot;
- pilot evidence supports an explicit G5 decision;
- portfolio, evidence, financial and risk records are complete and reviewable;
- one repeatable commercial package is tested;
- the next venture is selected by portfolio evidence rather than enthusiasm alone.

## 8. Failure and recovery rules

- Missing owner: hold the initiative.
- Missing evidence for two consecutive reviews: return to the previous gate or stop.
- Budget variance beyond threshold: freeze new commitments pending sponsor decision.
- Critical security, legal, safety, privacy or ethics issue: stop affected activity and escalate.
- Pilot sponsor withdrawal: preserve learning, reassess segment access and do not continue building by default.
- Excessive customization: return to MVP boundary and test a standardized configuration.

## 9. Post-pilot sequence

Only after the EL-VECTOR decision should PEFY-GG authorize the next strategic option for the same controlled process. The preferred next candidate is selected using strategic fit, evidence, market access, capital intensity, synergy and execution capacity—not by predetermined order.
