# VentureFoundry OS™ — System Specification

## 1. Purpose

VentureFoundry OS is a venture creation, validation, execution and scale management system. It converts entrepreneurial knowledge into a controlled operating cycle with explicit evidence, decisions, owners, metrics and risk controls.

It can be used for:

- a new standalone startup;
- an internal corporate venture;
- a product or business-unit incubation portfolio;
- a social-enterprise or foundation initiative;
- a digital, service, industrial, hardware or hybrid venture.

## 2. Core architecture

The system contains six integrated engines.

### 2.1 Strategy engine

Defines the venture mandate, strategic fit, target outcomes, market thesis, constraints and sponsor expectations.

**Minimum records:**

- venture charter;
- opportunity thesis;
- strategic-fit assessment;
- stakeholder map;
- boundary and exclusion statement;
- initial investment envelope.

### 2.2 Evidence engine

Turns assumptions into testable statements and records the evidence required for a decision.

**Minimum records:**

- assumption register;
- experiment cards;
- interview notes;
- evidence links;
- learning log;
- decision log.

### 2.3 Product and delivery engine

Defines the smallest reliable value loop, builds it, releases it and assigns operating ownership.

**Minimum records:**

- product brief;
- user journeys;
- MVP scope and exclusions;
- architecture decision records;
- acceptance criteria;
- release checklist;
- service ownership and support model.

### 2.4 Market and growth engine

Establishes a repeatable route from identified customer to activated, retained and economically valuable user.

**Minimum records:**

- ideal customer profile;
- positioning and message architecture;
- channel experiments;
- sales pipeline;
- onboarding funnel;
- retention cohorts;
- pricing and revenue evidence.

### 2.5 Management-system engine

Provides policies, roles, controls, reviews and assurance appropriate to the venture's maturity and exposure.

**Minimum records:**

- governance model;
- RACI;
- risk and opportunity register;
- compliance obligations register;
- information classification;
- financial controls;
- review calendar;
- corrective-action log.

### 2.6 Portfolio command engine

Allows leadership to compare ventures objectively and allocate capital, talent and attention.

**Minimum records:**

- portfolio register;
- weighted scorecard;
- dependency map;
- investment decision log;
- stop, hold, continue and scale decisions;
- benefits-realization dashboard.

## 3. Lifecycle and decision gates

### G0 — Mandate

**Question:** Is there a legitimate reason, sponsor and boundary for the venture?

**Pass conditions:** named sponsor; accountable venture owner; strategic fit; problem domain; target outcomes; resource ceiling; confidentiality classification.

### G1 — Opportunity

**Question:** Is the problem urgent, valuable and timely for a defined segment?

**Pass conditions:** at least one verified priority segment; documented pain; current alternatives; why-now rationale; credible market-access path.

### G2 — Solution

**Question:** Is the proposed value proposition desirable, feasible, viable and responsible?

**Pass conditions:** prototype or service simulation; technical feasibility; operating feasibility; legal and ethical review; testable value proposition.

### G3 — Validation

**Question:** Does observed behaviour support the commercial or impact hypothesis?

**Pass conditions:** evidence from interviews and behaviour; commitments or pilots; willingness-to-pay or equivalent value exchange; critical assumptions reduced.

### G4 — MVP

**Question:** Can the smallest value loop be delivered safely and reliably?

**Pass conditions:** working MVP; release criteria met; data/security baseline; owner and support process; measurement instrumentation.

### G5 — Traction

**Question:** Are acquisition, activation, retention and revenue or impact improving coherently?

**Pass conditions:** segment-level funnel; cohort retention; usage quality; customer success evidence; revenue quality; acceptable incident rate.

### G6 — Scale

**Question:** Is growth repeatable without destroying quality, cash, trust or control?

**Pass conditions:** positive or improving unit economics; repeatable channel; delivery capacity; documented processes; risk treatment; hiring and capital plan.

### G7 — Institutionalize

**Question:** Can the venture survive beyond founders and pilot conditions?

**Pass conditions:** delegated authority; audited records; resilient operations; management review; continuous improvement; portfolio integration or standalone governance.

## 4. Roles

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Executive Sponsor | Mandate, capital envelope, escalation and final gate decisions |
| Venture Owner | End-to-end results, evidence quality and decision preparation |
| Product Lead | User value, product scope, roadmap and adoption |
| Growth/Commercial Lead | Market access, pipeline, conversion, pricing and revenue |
| Delivery/Technology Lead | Architecture, build, release, reliability and technical debt |
| Finance Lead | budget, runway, unit economics, controls and forecasts |
| Risk/Compliance Lead | obligations, risks, controls, evidence and assurance |
| Customer/Impact Lead | customer outcomes, support, inclusion and impact measurement |
| AI Execution Team | research, synthesis, analysis, drafting and automation under assigned human approval |

Small ventures may combine roles, but accountabilities must remain explicit.

## 5. Weekly operating rhythm

1. Update evidence, metrics, risks and cash position.
2. Review the previous week's commitments.
3. Identify the single highest-risk assumption.
4. Approve no more than three priority experiments or delivery outcomes.
5. Assign owner, due date, expected evidence and decision consequence.
6. Escalate blocked dependencies within 24 hours.
7. Record continue, change, stop or scale decisions.

## 6. Monthly portfolio review

Each venture submits:

- current gate and confidence score;
- top five metrics;
- cash used, cash committed and runway;
- validated and invalidated assumptions;
- material risks and control status;
- customer evidence;
- next funding or resource request;
- explicit decision requested from leadership.

## 7. Evidence quality hierarchy

From weakest to strongest:

1. internal opinion;
2. desk research;
3. stated customer preference;
4. observed customer behaviour;
5. commitment of time, data, reputation or access;
6. signed pilot, pre-order or payment;
7. repeated usage and retention;
8. repeatable revenue or independently verified impact.

Gate decisions should rely on the strongest available evidence, not the largest volume of documents.

## 8. AI operating model

AI may accelerate research, synthesis, content, analysis, testing and workflow automation. It may not become the unnamed owner of a decision.

Every AI-assisted output must have:

- a defined purpose;
- an accountable human owner;
- source and evidence traceability where material;
- confidentiality handling;
- validation proportional to risk;
- version and approval status.

## 9. Minimum viable governance by maturity

| Maturity | Governance minimum |
|---|---|
| Concept | charter, owner, assumptions, confidentiality |
| Validation | experiment protocol, consent/privacy handling, decision log |
| MVP | release approval, security baseline, support and incident path |
| Traction | financial controls, customer terms, KPI integrity, risk register |
| Scale | delegated authority, audits, resilience, supplier and workforce controls |
| Institutional | integrated management reviews, assurance programme and improvement system |

## 10. Versioning

- **V0.x:** experimental design and pilot learning;
- **V1.x:** controlled operational use;
- **V2.x:** multi-venture portfolio use;
- **V3.x:** automated and integrated platform implementation.

The PEFY-GG implementation in this repository is the V1 pilot architecture.
